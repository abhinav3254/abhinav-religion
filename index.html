By The Numbers

WHY MOST NEVER
COME BACK

8,000m
Death Zone Begins
Above this altitude the human body cannot acclimatise. Oxygen is 33% of sea level. Brain damage begins in minutes. The body digests its own muscle tissue for fuel.
-60°C
Wind Chill on Summit
Exposed skin freezes in under 60 seconds. Frostbite takes fingers, toes, ears, nose — in that order. Some climbers have descended Everest with all their toes amputated at base camp.
200km/h
Jet Stream Winds
The jet stream sits directly over the Himalaya in winter. It shreds tents, hurls climbers off ridges, and can close a summit window permanently within 20 minutes.
32%
Annapurna Death Rate
1 in 3 people who summited Annapurna historically did not return alive. No other 8,000 m peak comes close. Modern safety has reduced this — but it remains the most lethal ratio in mountaineering.
200+
Bodies Left on Everest
Recovery is near-impossible above 8,000 m. The dead remain where they fell. Some have been there since 1924. They have names. They are landmarks on the route to the summit.
4–6hrs
Summit Weather Window
Miss it, and you descend in darkness through the Death Zone. Every minute above 8,000 m is irreversible biological damage. Cells die. They do not come back.
HACE
High-Altitude Cerebral Edema
The brain swells with fluid. Hallucinations, inability to stand, loss of judgement. Climbers have walked off ridges in a daze. It kills within hours if descent is not immediate.
Feb'26
Deep Winter — Most Dangerous
February is peak winter in the Himalaya. Fewer than 20 people have ever summited Everest in winter total. The cold and wind in February at altitude are beyond human imagination.

If You Want to Return

HOW TO NOT
DIE UP THERE

Acclimatise or Die
Ascend no more than 300–500 m per day above 3,000 m. Climb high, sleep low. Ignore this rule and your brain swells. HACE is not survivable above base camp. The mountain does not wait for you to feel ready.
Layer or Freeze
February EBC temperatures hit -30°C at night. Above 5,000 m with wind chill: -50°C. Full down suit, vapor-barrier socks, VBL gloves. Any exposed skin freezes in under 2 minutes near the summit.
Hydrate Obsessively
High altitude, cold, and dry air cause extreme fluid loss. You won't feel thirsty. Drink 4–6 litres daily above 4,000 m. Dehydration at altitude accelerates AMS, HACE, and HAPE — all fatal without descent.
Turn Around. Always.
80% of Himalayan deaths occur on descent. Set a hard turnaround time regardless of summit proximity. If you miss it, you descend. No discussion. No exceptions. The mountain will be there. You may not be.
Communication Is Life
Satellite phone, PLB beacon, VHF radio at every camp. Check in every 3 hours above base camp. In February, rescue helicopters cannot fly above 6,000 m. Below that ceiling, you are entirely alone.
Never Trust Clear Sky
The Himalaya in winter builds life-ending storms in 2 hours. Pallavi watches the pressure and jet-stream data every 4 hours. If she says abort, the team aborts. Immediately. No debate. No exceptions.
Know the Symptoms
Persistent headache, vomiting, loss of coordination, pink frothy cough — these are emergencies. Descend 1,000 m immediately. Dexamethasone and Nifedipine in every kit. Do not wait to see if it gets better. It won't.
Never Solo. Never.
Above 4,000 m all five move as one unit. Altitude impairs decision-making in ways you cannot detect in yourself. You need someone watching your symptoms. The mountain kills lone wolves before anyone else.
